What the published studies add

Reliance is widespread, but a written rule, use in practice and faster approval are three different observations. We retrieved supporting materials and compared published country findings with our national-text archive.

Results that change how we code the panel

  • Survey participation versus proven EMA use. 70 is the scope of the industry survey. EMA use for initial MAA met the five-company response threshold in 26 countries; these are different denominators.
  • Operational use without a legal framework. The supporting table labels Tunisia and Morocco this way; legal adoption and reliance in practice need separate variables.
  • Formal route versus implementation. Turkey is labelled not applied in practice in the supporting table.
  • Historical change. The 2021 regional study reports Brazil as no reliance in its 2019/2020 cross-section; our later 2024 Anvisa text concerns a changed regime.
  • Regional duplication. The 2021 study treats CARPHA/CRS as one framework covering 15 states; it does not supply 15 independent national enactment dates.
  • Underlying laws available by request. The 2021 article states the regulatory documents will be fully available by request. Its published tables are preserved, but a private source bundle is not claimed downloaded; no authors contacted.
  • Authority versus country. The 21-authority African survey includes Tanzania and Zanzibar as separate regulators. Nineteen country codes are matched and one additional Congo label is unresolved; 21 cannot be interpreted as 21 distinct countries.
  • Published supporting citation error. The APAC response file names Vietnam Circular 12/2025/TT-BYT but links to a TT-BTC finance/travel-expense circular. Preserve the response, exclude that hyperlink as drug-law evidence.
  • Case-study arithmetic and censoring. The 2026 oncology pilot retains Nigeria 7 versus 3 months and zero printed saving, six non-confirmed participants and two pending cases. We do not use its selected averages as a causal coefficient.
  • Country scope and historical changes. The 2025 industry route table labels Indonesia new indications only; the 2026 APAC survey includes NDAs. Scope and the later 2025 amendment need alignment.
  • A literature lead closes a direct-evidence gap. Colombia: recovered official Decree 677/1995 Articles 27–29 establish a two-reference-country route and a shorter pharmacological stage. Other domestic registration stages remain. Current status is stored alongside the preserved 71-jurisdiction baseline comparison.
  • Referenced document versus described instrument. The September 2026 review links Costa Rica to MS-DM-4910-2025, a signature-delegation agreement, while describing different resolutions. The original PDF is saved and is not used as the described pharmaceutical reliance rule.

Research implications

The strongest economic angle is the gap between legal permission and usable access to another regulator’s work. A rule may save scientific assessment work while adding document costs or leaving a national decision queue. These are distinct mechanisms that can be measured separately.

Our legal-wording comparisons identify these requirements. The published surveys add implementation evidence. Estimating their effect on entry, review time or prices requires linking a dated rule to eligible applications and their actual review stages; published pilot targets and selected averages are not substituted for that linkage.
